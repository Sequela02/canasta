# Evidence, Voice, and Locale Intake

Use this reference when the prompt does not already contain enough audience, evidence, voice, or locale context.

## Compact intake

Create a working table before drafting:

| Item                | Authorized source                                         | What it can support                          | What it cannot support                                      |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Product behavior    | Shipped UI, acceptance criteria, maintained docs          | Concrete capability and workflow             | Customer outcome or reliability guarantee                   |
| Audience vocabulary | Interviews, sales calls, support themes, search queries   | Terms, objections, level of technical detail | Representative market claim or testimonial without approval |
| Commercial claim    | Approved case study, measurement, pricing or policy owner | Claim at the documented scope and confidence | Broader guarantee or implied universal result               |
| Voice               | Repository voice guide and approved examples              | Register, rhythm, humor, formality           | Another brand's metaphors or a generic “human” voice        |
| Locale              | Locale guide, glossary, in-market review                  | Natural target-market wording and formats    | Invented regional slang or legal approval                   |

Record source locations or links. Keep quotations private unless publication rights are explicit.

## Claim-strength ladder

Use the narrowest truthful form:

1. **Observed behavior:** what the product demonstrably does.
2. **Documented capability:** what maintained product evidence authorizes.
3. **Measured result:** the result, population, method, and limits are published.
4. **Customer statement:** approved quotation with identity and scope.
5. **Guarantee or commitment:** only the contractual, policy, legal, security, or commercial owner may authorize it.

Do not slide upward because stronger language sounds better.

## Voice axes

Describe voice with evidence-bearing axes rather than one adjective:

- formal ↔ familiar;
- restrained ↔ energetic;
- editorial ↔ operational;
- plain-language ↔ domain-technical;
- literal ↔ playful;
- compact ↔ explanatory.

A brand can occupy different points by surface. A premium marketing page may be editorial while its error messages stay operational and literal.

## `es-MX` review packet

Include the intended audience in Mexico, product glossary, preferred pronouns and register, terms that remain untranslated, date/number/currency behavior, screenshots at target widths, and every consequential claim beside its English counterpart and evidence. Review semantic equivalence first and sentence-level resemblance last.
