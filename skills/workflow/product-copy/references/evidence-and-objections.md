# Evidence and Objections

## Claim-bound proof

Place the most relevant authorized evidence near the claim it supports. A generic logo wall, metric strip, or testimonial block does not prove every statement on the page.

For each material claim, record:

- claim and intended audience;
- factual owner;
- evidence type and source;
- authorization or privacy limit;
- freshness or date sensitivity;
- allowed wording and prohibited inference.

Useful evidence can include an attributable case, product capture, public repository, release artifact, documented process, benchmark with a valid comparison contract, or an operator-approved policy. Visual polish is not evidence.

## Source-to-claim inference

Judge evidence by claim fit, authority, freshness, and allowed inference:

| Source                                 | Can usually support                                                | Does not support by itself                                             |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Current repository or release artifact | Artifact existence, public scope, documented behavior, or maturity | Customer outcomes, adoption, reliability in use, or commercial success |
| Authorized case or operator record     | The bounded event, result, and context it records                  | A universal result or a stronger claim than the record contains        |
| Competitor or reference page           | Category language, conventions, and comparative hypotheses         | Causal conversion claims or permission to copy positioning             |
| Operator statement                     | Intent and an explicitly owned current decision                    | External market truth or an unverified technical property              |
| Visual polish                          | Presentation quality                                               | Product effectiveness, trustworthiness, or business impact             |

One successful pilot establishes local feasibility. Treating its result as a
cross-project standard still requires held-out evaluation or explicit operator
policy.

## Commercial content contract

Require explicit authority before publishing:

- pricing or minimum budgets;
- timelines, availability, support, or response commitments;
- guarantees or quantified outcomes;
- testimonials, client names, logos, or private work;
- legal incorporation, geography, tax, privacy, or compliance claims;
- security claims or statements about customer data.

Omit or mark the chapter blocked when authority is absent. Do not fill the gap with plausible copy.

## Objection handling

Derive objections from customer conversations, sales or support records, search behavior, operator evidence, or an explicitly labeled hypothesis. Decide whether each objection belongs:

- beside the claim that causes it;
- in qualification or process content;
- in a disclosure or comparison;
- in a consolidated FAQ because grouping improves findability.

Do not create FAQ entries to satisfy a count. Do not duplicate answers already visible on the page. Structured FAQ data needs a current interoperability or search purpose and separate technical ownership.

## Confidence

Separate:

- high confidence in logical completeness or information prerequisites;
- medium confidence from market conventions and expert review;
- low confidence in conversion impact without behavioral or voice-of-customer evidence.
